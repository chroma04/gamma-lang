The Cat and the Moon

The cat looked up at the moon, and the moon looked down at the cat.
"You're very small," said the moon. "And you're very far away,"
said the cat. Neither of them was wrong, and neither was sorry.

The cat had a habit of sitting on the wall at night. The wall was
cold, but the cat did not mind the cold. A cat that sits on a wall
sees many things: the lamps going out one by one, the baker's light
coming on, the first bird shaking the dew from its wings.

"Why do you stay up so late?" asked the moon.
"Because the night is the only time you visit," said the cat.
"And besides, the mice are out."

The moon laughed, which made the whole street brighter for a moment.
A dog barked twice and then thought better of it. The baker opened
his door, saw the cat on the wall, and put out a saucer of milk,
as he did every morning, and the cat, as it did every morning,
pretended not to notice until he had gone back inside.

By dawn the moon was pale and thin, a coin left on a blue table.
"Goodbye," said the cat. "Same time tomorrow?"
"If the clouds allow it," said the moon, and the cat went home.
